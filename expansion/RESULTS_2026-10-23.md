# Possible expansion: results (2026-10-23)

What the `possible-expansion` tools found when they were run on real data, and what changed because of it. Why the
tools exist: [README.md](README.md). The README's "How to implement" steps 3, 4 and 7 are done here; step 5 (era test)
is a workflow; step 6 (event study first) is checked in [§4](#4-event-study-does-it-tell-a-winner-from-a-loser).

## Data

- **5-year history:** the release `swarm-history-v1` (Binance USD-M 1-minute bars, funding, open interest), the **60
  coins** that spent longest in the point-in-time universe (BTC, ETH, SOL, AVAX, ADA, XRP, BNB, FIL, LINK, LTC, DOGE,
  NEAR, BCH, DOT, SHIB, UNI, TRX, AAVE, CRV, ETC, … SEI). Trades counted 2021-10-01 → 2026-09-30; each coin trades only
  on days it was in the universe (the sweep's gate). BloFin costs, funding charged.
- **Code path:** `history/sweep.js` (features → regime → plugin → ExecSim), now with `--frames` and
  `--breakeven both`, which runs every variant with and without break-even in the same pass. That way the comparison
  is the same ideas on the same bars.
- **Halves:** H1 = entries before 2024-04-01, H2 = after. t-stats on daily sums (trades on the same day move together).
- **Not reachable from the coding session:** Binance's archive and APIs (and every other exchange API) are blocked by
  this environment's network policy. So the 2017–2021 era test runs on a GitHub runner instead: the
  `swarm-era.yml` workflow (§5).

## 1. Break-even at +1 R: it costs the robust strategies, so paper now matches shadow (break-even off)

The paper and demo traders moved stops to entry + fees at +1 R; ExecSim (backtests, sweep, shadow) did not. Same
ideas, both ways:

| Variant (live name) | n | avg R as shadow | avg R with break-even | t (shadow) | sum R shadow → with |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ma-retest@720 (`ma-retest-12h`) | 1,436 | **+0.183** | +0.133 | 3.17 | 263 → 205 |
| ma-retest@1440 (`ma-retest-1d`) | 672 | **+0.219** | +0.185 | 2.51 | 147 → 133 |
| rsi-macd-trend@720 (`rsi-macd-trend-12h`) | 4,066 | **+0.123** | +0.113 | 2.52 | 499 → 480 |
| ma-retest@240 (`ma-retest-4h`) | 4,678 | +0.047 | +0.046 | 1.18 | 219 → 230 |
| ma-pullback@720 (`ma-pullback-12h`) | 3,879 | **+0.056** | +0.036 | 1.41 | 217 → 150 |

As one book (the five together, 1 R each):

| | Sum R | Sharpe | Max DD (R) | Return / DD |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| As shadow (no break-even) | **+1,345** | **1.33** | 231 | **5.83** |
| With break-even (as paper traded) | +1,198 | 1.24 | 214 | 5.59 |

H1 / H2: without +690 / +655 R, with +495 / +703 R. Break-even wins H2 by a little and loses H1 by a lot.

**Why:** with break-even, about 2,100 more trades end at about 0 R (stop-outs fall from −1.02 to −0.72 R on
average). They come from all three exits: 384 fewer trades reach the +2 R target (each one is about −2 R against
the book) and 750 fewer run to the time stop, where trades averaged +0.4 R. The losers saved do not make up for it. The
deep review's hint, that trades reaching +1 R end at +1.05–1.13 R on average, pointed this way.

**Change:** `config/swarm.json` → `trading.breakevenR: 0`. Paper and demo now exit exactly like shadow and the
backtests, so the promotion gate's comparison (shadow vs backtest) also describes what paper does. This is a
`trading` section change: the PC's update gate holds it for Ibrahim's approval. To go back, set it to 1; to try it
on one strategy only, use the sweep's `--breakeven both` first.

## 2. adaptive-score (the 1-minute bot's strategy on slow bars): no edge, stays off

| Variant | n | avg R | at 1.5× costs | t | H1 | H2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| adaptive-score@240 | 21,000 | −0.009 | −0.024 | −0.38 | −0.024 | +0.007 |
| adaptive-score@720 | 6,624 | +0.024 | +0.016 | 0.56 | +0.028 | +0.019 |
| adaptive-score@1440 | 3,113 | +0.036 | +0.030 | 0.65 | +0.053 | +0.017 |

For comparison, rsi-macd-trend@720 (the same kind of trend + momentum idea) makes +0.123 R at t 2.5. The factory's
bar is t ≥ 2 + 0.5·log10(tries); adaptive-score is nowhere near even t 1. The README's own rule applies: "if it adds nothing beyond rsi-macd-trend, retire
it". The plugin stays registered (the sweep group can be rerun), and its three config entries stay `enabled: false`
with the verdict in their notes. **6 tries** to log in `swarm-factory/trials.jsonl` (the three timeframes, with and
without break-even).

## 3. Loss-streak rule (half size after 3 losses, skip after 6): costs more than it saves, not adopted

| Strategy | Longest losing run (chance ≈) | Without: sum R / DD / R÷DD | With the rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| ma-retest@720 | 25 (11) | 263 / 61 / 4.31 | 182 / 53 / 3.42 |
| ma-retest@1440 | 23 (9) | 147 / 35 / 4.21 | 133 / 29 / 4.62 |
| rsi-macd-trend@720 | 31 (13) | 499 / 87 / 5.74 | 337 / 84 / 4.00 |
| ma-retest@240 | 42 (15) | 219 / 116 / 1.89 | 189 / 88 / 2.15 |
| ma-pullback@720 | 41 (14) | 217 / 144 / 1.51 | 180 / 137 / 1.31 |

It gives up 10–32% of the R and improves return-to-drawdown in two of five. Losing runs *are* about twice as long as
the chance level, because with 60 coins open at once a market turn stops out many trades together. But a run of
losses says nothing about the next trade, so cutting size after one does not help.

**A bug in the replay tool, fixed here:** `streak_rules.js` sized each trade from the trades that closed before *its
exit*. With many coins open at once, that "skips" trades already open when a cluster of correlated stops hits. That's
look-ahead, and it made the rule look brilliant: R÷DD 17–41 instead of 1.3–4.6. The size is now fixed when the trade
opens, from trades already closed by then. There's a new test case with seven overlapping losers.

## 4. Event study: does it tell a winner from a loser?

The README proposes `event_study.js` as the cheap screen before any backtest. To check that it works, it was run on
two strategies known to work, two known to fail, and adaptive-score. Data: 12 majors (BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP, DOGE, ADA,
LINK, LTC, AVAX, BNB, DOT, NEAR), 2021-10 → 2026-09. Forward R after the signal, in the idea's own bars; edge = average
MFE ÷ |average MAE|.

| Strategy | Ideas | Forward R at 6 / 12 / 24 bars (t at 12) | Edge at 12 / 24 bars | Forward R at 12 bars, H1 / H2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ma-retest-12h (works) | 387 | +0.10 / **+0.23** / +0.50 (t 3.1) | **1.31 / 1.56** | +0.20 / +0.27 |
| rsi-macd-trend-12h (works) | 980 | +0.14 / **+0.24** / +0.18 (t 3.7) | **1.33 / 1.24** | +0.30 / +0.17 |
| candle-engulfing-4h (fails) | 2,885 | −0.13 / −0.12 / −0.08 (t −2.7) | 0.85 / 0.90 | −0.15 / −0.09 |
| wyckoff-spring-4h (fails) | 1,578 | +0.03 / +0.05 / +0.08 (t 0.95) | 0.98 / 0.98 | −0.02 / +0.11 |
| adaptive-score-4h | 3,031 | −0.01 / +0.00 / +0.09 (t 0.03) | 1.12 / 1.12 | +0.02 / −0.02 |
| adaptive-score-12h | 999 | +0.07 / +0.22 / +0.18 (t 2.6) | 1.18 / 1.10 | **+0.38 / +0.07** |

**It works as a screen.** The strategies that work have an edge of 1.3–1.6 and forward R that grows with the horizon
in both halves. The ones that fail sit at or below 1 (the deep review's "MFE ≈ MAE"). adaptive-score is in between:
some excursion edge, but its forward R comes from H1 and fades in H2 (12h: +0.38 → +0.07; 24 bars: +0.59 → −0.24).
That matches its backtest. So the rule for the researcher: **edge ≥ ~1.2 and forward R positive in both halves
before a factory candidate is written.**

Two caveats. The t-stats here are per idea, and ideas on different coins on the same day move together, so read them
as optimistic (the backtests above use daily sums). The `adverse_first` tag is an outcome, so its huge "lift" is by
construction; never use it as a filter. The tag tables found nothing else with |t| ≥ 2 except long vs short, which is
regime again (§5).

## 5. Long and short: both sides earn, so no side filter

Over 2021-10 → 2026-09 the short side made most of the R, which raises the question of a long-only or short-only
filter. By year it is regime, not a side bias. ma-retest@720: longs −34 R in 2022 and −34 in 2025 (falling markets),
+71 in 2023 and +41 in 2024 (rising); shorts the mirror image. rsi-macd-trend@720 and ma-pullback@720 show the same
pattern. These are two-sided trend followers; a side filter would be fitted to which way 2021–26 went.

The era test is the check on that. `.github/workflows/swarm-era.yml` (by hand: Actions → Swarm era test → Run)
downloads Binance spot 1-minute archives for BTC and 11 other majors, 2017-08 → 2021-09, with checksums verified.
That covers the 2018 bear market, 2019, and the 2020–21 bull run that none of the swarm's data includes. It imports
them with `tools/import_binance_1m.js`, backtests the robust five, donchian-1d-20 and adaptive-score with and without
break-even, replays the streak rule, and publishes the report to `swarm-reports` under `era/<date>/`. It's spot data,
so there's no funding: use it for direction and stability, not costs.
